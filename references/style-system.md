# Shared style system

## 1. Whole-image common ground

The defining effect is **real-scene photography plus flat hand-drawn narrative intervention**.

- The real photograph remains the base layer and supplies location, light, depth, perspective, and material detail.
- The illustrated layer stays visibly graphic. It is not color-graded into photorealism and does not attempt to imitate skin, fabric, or atmospheric texture exactly.
- The visual interest comes from the contrast between photographic detail and simplified drawing.
- The new subject changes the story of the place through scale, action, or fantasy—not merely decoration.
- The scene uses real geometry: rails become seats, roofs become platforms, streets become paths, water becomes an activity surface, and buildings become objects to lean on, look over, or emerge from.
- Occlusion is essential. Place parts of the illustration in front of and, when plausible, behind real objects.
- Exaggerated scale is welcome, but the camera angle and contact points must remain coherent.
- Large clear shapes dominate. Small doodles, sparkles, clouds, flowers, arrows, motion marks, or handwritten words are secondary.
- Preserve negative space. Do not fill every empty region with illustration.
- A soft, slightly faded photograph works for gentle stories; a crisp or desaturated photograph works for high-color urban/editorial interventions. Avoid destroying highlight and shadow detail.

### Composition modes

Choose the lightest mode that expresses the idea:

1. **Single character intervention:** one clear subject and one scene interaction.
2. **Companion vignette:** a smaller character, animal, or quiet corner narrative.
3. **World expansion:** several characters or doodles distributed around a landmark or aerial scene.
4. **Editorial poster:** giant figure plus typography, frame, title, or campaign-like layout. Use only when the user wants a poster or promotional result.

## 2. Illustrated character common ground

### Shape language

- Construct the body from rounded geometric masses: broad curves, capsules, soft rectangles, circles, and tapered limbs.
- Favor an immediately readable silhouette over anatomical accuracy.
- Exaggerate one or two proportions: long legs, oversized trousers, broad sleeves, large hands, compact head, broad torso, or oversized shoes.
- Keep poses expressive and slightly theatrical. The line of action should be easy to trace.
- Simplify hands to mitten-like or rounded graphic shapes when detail is unnecessary.

### Face and identity

- Use a small face relative to the body.
- Build features from minimal marks: dot or almond eyes, a small nose line, a short mouth curve, and simple brows.
- Keep expressions warm, curious, calm, playful, or surprised rather than hyper-detailed.
- Hair reads as one or a few bold masses, sometimes with a few texture marks.
- Vary skin tone, body type, age presentation, hair, and styling intentionally. Do not make gender presentation depend on stereotypes.

### Clothing

- Clothing carries much of the character design: oversized jackets, wide trousers, loose sweaters, dresses, sportswear, sneakers, hats, bags, or activity props.
- Use two to four dominant colors with one accent. High-contrast urban palettes can use yellow, pink, green, blue, orange, purple, black, or cream.
- For gentle scenes, use dusty blue, lavender, warm white, muted red, sage, peach, or charcoal.
- Use flat fills first. Add sparse pencil hatching, crayon grain, checks, stripes, or scribbles as secondary texture.
- Keep folds and seams minimal; describe volume through silhouette and color blocking.

### Line and finish

- Use a clean but human line: slightly uneven, mostly consistent in weight, and never mechanically perfect.
- Black or dark colored outlines work for lively editorial scenes; pale or partial outlines work for quiet scenes.
- Do not shade realistically. If depth is needed, use one flat shadow shape or a subtle grain layer.
- Preserve a collage-like boundary between drawing and photograph.

### Character families supported

- **Urban giant:** bold fashion, dynamic gesture, architectural interaction.
- **Gentle dreamer:** softer palette, calm action, nature or sky motifs.
- **Everyday companion:** smaller scale, transit or domestic behavior, restrained detail.
- **Playful explorer:** bicycle, camera, telescope, map, fishing rod, or travel prop.
- **Animal or creature:** simple iconic anatomy, expressive face, strong readable contour.
- **Cinematic monster:** dramatic scale, warning colors, energetic effects; reserve for explicitly absurd or action-oriented requests.

These are decision aids, not named artist imitations. Never frame the result as copying a living artist's signature style.

## Failure patterns

- A centered character pasted into unused space without touching the environment.
- Photorealistic skin or fabric that erases the illustration/photo contrast.
- Clothing that contradicts obvious weather or physical activity without narrative purpose.
- Excessive stickers and doodles that flatten the photographic setting.
- Tiny facial detail paired with an unreadable body silhouette.
- Incorrect ground contact, floating feet, impossible hand grips, or missing occlusion.
- A generic character menu unrelated to the uploaded photograph.
